\section{Testing}
    \par Ideally, during the development of this project, a test plan would have been applied to verify the usefulness of this kind of system. However, due to time constraints, extensive testing was not achievable. However, self-directed testing of the system was performed on two occasions, each using different iterations of the \gls{hud} interface.

    \subsection{Usability Tests}
        \subsubsection{Test 1 (Sunday, November 10th, 2024)}
        \par Usability Test 1 occurred on Sunday, November 10th, 2024, starting at 3:10 PM \gls{cst}. The test ran for approximately 7 minutes. The first \gls{hud} interface iteration (Figure \ref{fig:dasharhuduiversion1}) was tested on public roads in Auburn, Kentucky. A map of the route with directional indication is visible in Figure \ref{fig:dasharusabilitytest1map}.

        \begin{figure}[h!]
            \vspace{0.5cm}
            \centering
            \includegraphics[scale=0.3]{./Support/Images/DashAR-TestRoute-2024-11-10.png}
            \caption{DashAR Usability Test 1 Mapped Route}
            \label{fig:dasharusabilitytest1map}
        \end{figure}

        \par During the test, weather conditions were fair, and traffic conditions were normal for the area: light, and with minimal stopping. The route started in the parking lot of a local market. From there, the automobile was driven onto West Main Street. After brief continuation on that street, a right turn was made onto \glsxtrshort{us} Highway 68-80's eastbound lanes. The automobile traveled for 3 miles, then turned right onto East Main Street. The automobile drove another 3 miles down Main Street, through downtown Auburn, until the route was completed at the right-hand turn onto \gls{us} Highway 68-80, encountered previously.

        \par This first brief test proved successful. The system was able to update values in near real time, as the \gls{ar} subsystem polled the \gls{das} \gls{api} every five frames. The data displayed in the \gls{hud} interface was compared to those visible in the automobile's instrument cluster.

        \par The only widget that did not immediately update was the fuel level widget. This was due to the previously mentioned dilemma around fuel level calculations, resulting in a delayed calculation that only provided results after 1,000 calls to the \gls{api}. Under normal operation, the fuel level would be displayed as a hyphen until the first 1,000 \gls{api} calls could be made. However, before this usability testing formally began, the automobile ran with the system in place, allow the first 1,000 data points to be collected beforehand, resulting in a calculation of 20\% full. On the portion of the route down the 3 mile stretch of Main Street, the fuel level re-calculated, and displayed a drop of 1\%.

        \par An issue of note during this test was regarding the compass widget's output. Since the compass data is retrieved directly from the \gls{ar} subsystem's compute device, placement of this device within the cabin of the automobile was critical for successful operation. The device must be placed with the screen facing upwards, and with the \gls{usb}-C ports facing the rear of the automobile. This would allow the device's magnetometer to be oriented correctly, mimicking the direction of the automobile. Any other orientation would result in errant compass readings. An improvement that could be made to resolve this issue in the future would be to either capture the compass data from the \gls{ics} device, since it is natually fixed in place within the automobile, or by defining a specific setup configuration for how the \gls{ar} subsystem compute device should be oriented during operation.

        \par Another issue of note was related to the communication between the \gls{ar} subsystem and the \gls{ics} device. At some point after testing formally concluded, it was observed that the two subsystems stopped communicating, resulting in the \gls{hud} not updating. This, in turn, stopped the \gls{das} \gls{api} from collecting \gls{obdii} data from the automobile. Unfortunately, the issue was not reproducible after multiple attempts. However, it revealed a circumstance within the system that had not been explored deeply: error handling when communications fail.

        \par Ideally, if the subsystems fail to communicate with each other, over an extensive period of time, the \gls{ar} subsystem would clear the \gls{hud} interface from display, to prevent the user from interpreting the data as the current, instantaneous reading. Additionally, the \gls{hud} application would display an error to the user, in a non-invasive manner, stating there is a problem and the \gls{ar} glasses should be removed until the driver can safely stop the automobile.
