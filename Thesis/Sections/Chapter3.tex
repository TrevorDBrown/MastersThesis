\section{Summary}
    \par DashAR (pronounced either as it looks - a portmanteau of ``Dashboard'' and ``\gls{ar}'' - or like Dasher the Reindeer) is a dynamic \gls{hud} solution for drivers of automobiles. It provides drivers near real-time information, such as automobile's speed in space, the engine's rotational speed internally, the amount of fuel remaining in the fuel tank, and so on. These are pieces of information generally found on the instrument cluster of most automobiles.

\section{System Design}
    \par During the development of IBM's Watson platform - a massively parallel computation project in the early 2010s - Dr. Bijan Davari, \gls{ibm} Fellow and Vice President of Next Generation Computing Systems and Technology, stated, regarding application development at the scale of Watson that ``That application is the king. Start with that, what are the key requirements for that, and how do they need to be satisfied. You bring the elements that are required to take care of that application need.'' \cite{IBM2012} In other words, design the software first, then proceed to bring in the necessary resources as they are needed. That is the approach taken with this project.

    \par When starting out, a basic framework was designed that would handle the following basic functions:

    \begin{itemize}
        \item An \gls{ar} component, for rendering an automotive \gls{hud}.
        \item A computer, for capturing data from various feeds, like \gls{obdii} data and the internet.
        \item Middleware software, to run on the computer, that would aggregate and serve the data to the automotive \gls{hud}.
        \item A basic wireless network, for communication between devices.
        \item Interchangeable, system-agnostic components.
    \end{itemize}

    \par This framework led to the realization of the system components which comprise DashAR - the automobile, the \gls{ics}, and the \gls{ar} subsystem.

    \subsection{System Components}
        \subsubsection{Automobile}
            \par The automobile, being the core component of DashAR, is any gasoline powered automobile manufactured after 1996, or any diesel powered automobile after 1997. This is because they are required to have \gls{obdii} \cite{CEPA2019}. Considering that the mean age of an automobile is 12 years in the \glsxtrshort{usa} \cite{BTS2024} and 18 years in Western Europe \cite{Held2021}, it is safe to assume a wide array of people will be able to use DashAR as designed, with little to no modification.

        \subsubsection{Intermediate Computing Subsystem}
            \par The next major consideration made for the project was the method in which the \gls{ar} subsystem would communicate with the automobile. It was determined a computer would be needed that met the following criteria:

            \begin{itemize}
                \item Portable - the computer should be able to fit int he automobile easily. The smaller the computer, the better.
                \item Energy Consumption - the computer should be able to be powered by the automobile while driving.
                \item Computing Power - the computer should be able to process data from the automobile and other inputs in near real-time (i.e. within, at most, 500 ms/s).
                \item Headless Operation - the computer should be able to run without direct interaction from the user.
                \item Connectivity - the computer should be able to interface with \gls{usb} devices, Bluetooth devices, and WiFi networks.
            \end{itemize}

            \cite{RPL2025} produces the \gls{rpi} 5 - a \gls{sbc} released in 2023 - which meets all of the criteria prescribed. The \gls{rpi} 5 uses an industry standard, ARM-based processor, operating at 2.4 GHz, with \gls{ram} options between 2 GB to 16 GB. This is comparable to a early- to mid-2010s desktop \glsxtrshort{pc}. It is very compact physically, measuring in at 56mm by 85mm. It contains the necessary connectivity options that make it a feasible option for the \gls{ics}, as it can interface with the automobile via a serial-to-\gls{obdii} adapter, either wired (i.e. \gls{usb}) or wireless (i.e. Bluetooth). Regarding energy consumption, the \gls{rpi} 5 consumes around 12 watts of power over a \gls{usb}-C cable, requiring 5V/5A \gls{dc}.

        \subsubsection{AR Subsystem}
            \par Finally, the last major consideration made for the project was the method in which the \gls{hud} would be displayed. At the time the project started, Meta's Quest 3 \gls{ar} headset was the most commonplace \gls{ar} product that fit the application's need. However, due to the bulkiness of the headset, and the legal concerns regarding \gls{vr} utilization while operating an automobile, this product selection was quickly abandoned.

            \par While searching for alternative options, a relatively unknown product came to the forefront of this author's attention, which, on paper, seemed like an ideal solution to these concerns - the Air 2 line of \gls{ar} glasses by XREAL. The Air 2 Pro \gls{ar} glasses is a small form factor \gls{ar} solution that is approximately the size of a pair of large sunglasses.

            \par Since their founding in 2017, XREAL has released multiple models of their \gls{ar} glasses, as well as companion devices - the XREAL Beam and XREAL Beam Pro \cite{XREAL2025}.

            \par At the start of development, the Air 2 Pro \gls{ar} glasses were considered to be XREAL's mid-tier offering, as they either lacked certain functionality found in the higher end model or were limited in their function (e.g. front facing cameras, 6-\gls{dof} as opposed to the Air 2 Pro's 3-\gls{dof}, etc.)

\section{Why DashAR?}
    \par Considering the design of the DashAR system, a question that may come to someone's mind when thinking about this approach: ``Why can't I just look down at the instrument cluster in my car?''. Admittedly, for many people, that is the most practical solution. After all, it is available, and it is there to help the driver.

    \par However, the biggest problem today with the instrument cluster is the information presented is fixed. The instrument cluster can't easily be adjusted to meet the needs of every individual that operates a specific automobile. The information presented within an instrument cluster is also at the mercy of the manufacturer. With the DashAR System, the user has control over the information presented to them as they drive.
